Sept 1
Germany Invades Poland
Hitler's Wehrmacht crosses into Poland using the devastating Blitzkrieg doctrine — fast armor, air support, and encirclement. Britain and France declare war two days later. The Second World War has begun.

France Falls — The Armistice
After six catastrophic weeks, France surrenders. Germany occupies the north while the collaborationist Vichy regime rules the south. Churchill tells a stunned Britain: we shall never surrender.
Axis Victory
1941
June 22
Operation Barbarossa — Eastern Front Opens
Germany launches the largest invasion in military history: 3 million troops along a 2,900 km front into the Soviet Union. Hitler's catastrophic overreach would prove fatal to the Third Reich.
Axis Aggression
1941
Dec 7
Pearl Harbor — America Enters the War
Japan's surprise aerial attack on the US Pacific Fleet kills over 2,400 Americans. FDR calls it a date which will live in infamy. The US declares war on Japan; Germany declares war on the US.
Axis Aggression
1943
Feb 2
Stalingrad — The Turning Point
Germany's 6th Army surrenders after months of brutal urban combat at −30°C. Over 800,000 Axis soldiers are killed or captured. The momentum on the Eastern Front shifts irrevocably toward the Soviets.
Turning Point
1944
June 6
D-Day — The Normandy Landings
156,000 Allied troops storm five beaches on the French coast in the largest seaborne invasion in history. Operation Overlord cracks open the Western Front. Paris is liberated two months later.
Allied Advance
1945
May 8
V-E Day — Germany Surrenders
Nazi Germany surrenders unconditionally. Hitler died by suicide in his Berlin bunker one week prior. Europe erupts in celebration. The war in Europe is over — the Pacific still burns.
Allied Victory
1945
Aug 6–9
Hiroshima & Nagasaki — The Nuclear Age Begins
The US drops atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki, killing over 110,000 instantly. Japan surrenders on August 15, ending the deadliest war in human history and opening the age of nuclear warfare.

Protagonists

Primary
Figures

The war was shaped by the visions, obsessions, and decisions of a handful of individuals whose choices determined the fate of millions — for better and for devastating worse.

H
Germany
Adolf Hitler
Führer of the Third Reich, 1933–1945
Hitler's ideology of racial supremacy drove the Holocaust and plunged the world into war. His catastrophic military overreach — particularly the invasion of the USSR — sealed Germany's total defeat.
C
United Kingdom
Winston Churchill
Prime Minister, 1940–1945
Churchill's defiant oratory rallied Britain after France's fall. His masterful statecraft forged the Grand Alliance with the US and USSR, and his refusal to negotiate with Hitler proved decisive.
R
United States
Franklin D. Roosevelt
President of the United States, 1933–1945
FDR transformed the US into the Arsenal of Democracy and forged the Grand Alliance. He died in April 1945, weeks before Germany's surrender, never seeing the victory he spent four years building.
S
Soviet Union
Joseph Stalin
General Secretary, 1924–1953
Stalin's Red Army absorbed catastrophic losses before crushing Germany at Stalingrad and driving to Berlin. His iron rule extracted maximum sacrifice — and he emerged commanding half of Europe.
H
Empire of Japan
Emperor Hirohito
Emperor of Japan, 1926–1989
Japan's divine emperor presided over its imperial expansion. His unprecedented radio broadcast announcing Japan's surrender ended the war — his people's first hearing of his voice was a message of defeat.
M
Fascist Italy
Benito Mussolini
Il Duce, 1922–1943
Founder of fascism and Hitler's chief ally, Mussolini dreamed of a new Roman Empire. Defeated in North Africa, he was overthrown, rescued by Germany, and ultimately executed by Italian partisans.
D
Free France
Charles de Gaulle
Leader of Free France, 1940–1944
Refusing to accept defeat, de Gaulle broadcast his Appeal of June 18 from London. He led the Free French, restored French sovereignty, and went on to found the Fifth Republic.
E
Allied Forces
Dwight D. Eisenhower
Supreme Allied Commander, Europe
Eisenhower commanded Operation Overlord and the final Allied campaign into Germany. His genius was diplomatic as much as military — managing fractious coalitions under impossible pressure. Later the 34th US President.
04

Legacy

The World
Remade

🌐
United Nations Founded

The League of Nations' failure led to the UN in 1945, with 51 founding members committed to collective security and human rights. Its Security Council reflected the new balance of world power.

❄️
The Cold War

Victory left two superpowers facing each other across an iron curtain. The US-Soviet rivalry spawned proxy wars across Asia, Africa, and Latin America — dividing the world for 45 years.

✡️
Holocaust & Israel

The murder of six million Jews in the Holocaust accelerated the push for a Jewish homeland. The State of Israel was declared in 1948, permanently transforming the politics of the Middle East.

⚛️
The Nuclear Age

Hiroshima and Nagasaki inaugurated an era of existential risk. The nuclear arms race between the US and USSR produced enough warheads to destroy civilization — and birthed Mutually Assured Destruction.

🗺️
Decolonization

Weakened European empires could no longer hold their colonies. Within two decades, dozens of nations across Africa and Asia won independence, redrawing the world map and ending centuries of colonial rule.

⚖️
War Crimes Accountability

The Nuremberg and Tokyo trials established that heads of state could be held accountable for crimes against humanity — laying the bedrock of international law and the universal human rights framework.
